# Ownership defaults and recorded title

Public research IP does not automatically belong to the government, the university, or the inventor. Record what the instruments say.

## United States — Bayh-Dole baseline

Federal funding agreements with contractors (including universities and small businesses) are governed by 35 U.S.C. 200–212 and 37 C.F.R. 401.

Default public facts you may rely on without a private contract:

- The contractor may elect title to subject inventions.
- The government receives a non-exclusive, non-transferable, irrevocable, paid-up license to practice the invention or have it practiced for or on behalf of the United States.
- The US patent should carry a government-interest statement naming the agency and often the award.
- March-in rights exist in statute. A march-in that did not happen is not evidence of a policy conclusion. Do not editorialize.
- Nonprofit contractors have restrictions on assignment without agency approval.

If election, assignment back to the government, or waiver is not in a public record, leave those nodes `unresolved`.

SBIR/STTR data-rights and patent clauses differ from university grants. Do not copy Bayh-Dole university defaults onto a small-business contract.

## Assignment chain beats front page

Live owner ≠ assignee printed on the granted patent. Walk USPTO assignment records in date order. For each conveyance capture:

- assignor and assignee legal names
- conveyance type (assignment of assignor's interest, merger, security interest, name change, license — rare on this register)
- recorded date and purported execution date
- reel/frame

Stop the chain at the latest title-bearing conveyance. List later security interests as encumbrances, not owners.

## University and TTO layer

Typical public sequence, each hop still needing evidence:

1. Inventor duty-to-assign under employment / policy
2. Invention disclosure (usually private)
3. Institution elects title
4. Institution files or has counsel file
5. TTO markets
6. Option, exclusive license, non-exclusive license, or startup license + equity
7. Possible inter-institutional agreement when inventors span campuses

A TTO "available technologies" page is marketing. A spinout press release is marketing. EDGAR license exhibits and recorded assignments are title.

Inventor equity or royalty participation is almost never public at person-level. Institutional royalty is sometimes in PAC or foundation reports. If absent, `unresolved`, not zero.

## Non-US defaults (minimal)

- UK / many EU universities — institutional ownership is common by statute or employment contract, not identical to Bayh-Dole. Do not label it Bayh-Dole.
- Inventor-ownership jurisdictions exist. Confirm before asserting university title.
- Horizon / UKRI grants have their own result and access rules. Use CORDIS and the grant agreement family, not USPTO heuristics.

## What not to infer

- Exclusive license from "partnered with"
- University ownership from inventor email domain
- Government ownership from the word "sponsored"
- Clean title from a startup's pitch deck
